## About Me

I have always been curious about what lies beneath the surface—how things work, why patterns emerge, and how seemingly separate events may be connected. That curiosity led me to study physics and mathematics, where I learned to approach complex problems through evidence, logic, and fundamental principles.

Physics also taught me the value of simplicity. Simplicity does not mean ignoring complexity; it means identifying the relationships that matter and expressing them in a form others can understand. I believe the same principle lies at the heart of effective data analysis and visualization.

I first experienced the storytelling power of data while conducting biophotonics research at Washburn University. I analyzed complex microscopy images and interpreted patterns in the Fourier plane to understand physical and biological changes. Information that initially appeared abstract could reveal a meaningful story once it was properly processed, analyzed, and explained.

That experience shaped how I see data today. Data is more than a collection of numbers, measurements, or images—it is evidence left behind by an underlying process. An analyst’s role is to investigate that evidence, identify patterns and anomalies, and transform the findings into insights people can understand and use.

I enjoy exploring unfamiliar datasets, discovering hidden relationships, forecasting possible outcomes, and building clear visualizations. Whether an analysis reveals an emerging trend, an unexpected anomaly, or a concern hidden beneath summary figures, my goal is to understand what is happening, explain why it matters, and support better decisions.
